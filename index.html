Professional Self-Assessment

Throughout my time in the Computer Science program at Southern New Hampshire University, I have developed a stronger understanding of how software, data, and technology work together to solve problems. Over the past three years, I have improved my programming, problem-solving, and troubleshooting skills while gaining experience with software engineering, algorithms, data structures, databases, and security. Completing this ePortfolio has given me the opportunity to revisit previous work, recognize areas where I have improved, and demonstrate how I can apply what I have learned to real-world situations.

My professional background has also influenced how I approach computer science. I currently work as a field service technician for Siemens, where troubleshooting electrical equipment requires careful analysis, attention to detail, and an understanding of how different systems interact. As I continue developing my career, I hope to become more involved with programmable logic controllers (PLCs), protective relays, and industrial automation. The skills I have gained through this degree will help me better understand the software used to configure, monitor, and troubleshoot these systems.

One important lesson from the Computer Science program has been the value of communication and collaboration during software development. Working through project requirements, reviewing code, and considering feedback have helped me understand why developers need to communicate clearly with both technical and nontechnical audiences. A software solution may function correctly, but it also needs to be understandable, maintainable, and useful to the people who depend on it.

The CS 499 code review gave me an opportunity to explain the structure of an existing application, identify areas for improvement, and communicate the reasoning behind planned changes. This experience reinforced the importance of reviewing software from multiple perspectives and making design decisions that support the needs of users and other stakeholders. Throughout the program, I have learned how important software organization and maintainability are to developing reliable applications. Object-oriented programming, modular design, and separation of responsibilities have helped me understand how larger applications can be divided into smaller components that are easier to develop, test, and troubleshoot.

My CS 340 Grazioso Salvare project provided an opportunity to apply these concepts. The application uses Python and MongoDB to manage animal shelter information and display results through an interactive dashboard. For my capstone, I focused on improving the structure of the application by separating responsibilities, strengthening error handling, and making the code easier to maintain.

Studying algorithms and data structures helped me understand that solving a problem involves more than producing the correct result. The way data is organized, searched, and processed can significantly affect the performance and usefulness of an application. For my capstone, I expanded the original animal filtering functionality by developing a more advanced approach to evaluating rescue candidates. Instead of relying only on predefined filters, the enhancement uses multiple criteria to evaluate and rank animals based on their suitability for rescue training. This demonstrates how algorithmic techniques can improve the information available to users and support better decision-making.

Database development has been another important part of my education. I have gained experience working with structured and unstructured data, performing CRUD operations, and understanding how applications interact with databases. The Grazioso Salvare application uses MongoDB to store and retrieve animal shelter records. Improving the database functionality allowed me to focus on validation, query construction, error handling, and efficient data retrieval. These improvements also reinforced the importance of data integrity and reliable communication between application components.

My coursework has helped me understand that security needs to be considered throughout the software development process rather than treated as something added at the end. Input validation, proper exception handling, secure configuration management, and protecting sensitive information are all important parts of developing dependable applications. These principles were especially relevant while improving the Grazioso Salvare project. Strengthening the handling of database configuration, validating application inputs, and managing errors more consistently helped demonstrate how security and reliability can be incorporated into software design.

Completing this program has helped me develop a different perspective on troubleshooting and problem-solving. In my field service work, I often need to identify problems, understand how equipment and components interact, and determine the most effective solution. Computer science follows a similar process, but applies it to software, data, and computing systems. My long-term goal is to combine my field service experience with my computer science education to work more extensively with industrial automation, PLC programming, protective relay configuration, and software-based diagnostics. Having experience with both physical electrical systems and software development will help me approach technical problems from multiple perspectives.

This ePortfolio demonstrates my growth through three enhancements to the Grazioso Salvare application originally developed in CS 340. The Software Engineering and Design enhancement focuses on improving application architecture and maintainability. The Algorithms and Data Structures enhancement introduces a more advanced method for evaluating and ranking rescue candidates. The Databases enhancement improves how the application validates, retrieves, and manages data through MongoDB.

Together, these enhancements demonstrate my ability to evaluate existing software, identify opportunities for improvement, apply appropriate computing techniques, and consider security throughout development. The accompanying code review and enhancement narratives explain the decisions behind these improvements and reflect on the knowledge and skills I developed throughout the process. Completing this capstone has helped me recognize how much I have learned during the Computer Science program and how these skills can support my future career. More importantly, it has given me practical examples of my ability to develop, improve, and communicate software solutions that address real problems.
